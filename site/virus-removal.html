Pop-ups, fake warnings, a PC that's not acting right?

Viruses and unwanted programs slow your computer down, hijack your browser and try to scare you into paying for things you don't need. I clean them out properly and explain how they got in, so it's less likely to happen again.

Signs something's on there

  • Pop-ups or "your PC is infected" warnings that won't go away
  • Your homepage or search engine changed on its own
  • Programs you never installed
  • The PC suddenly got much slower, or the fan runs constantly
  • Friends are getting strange emails or messages from you

What I do

  • Find and remove the infection and the junk that came with it
  • Check startup programs and browser extensions for anything that shouldn't be there
  • Make sure Windows and its built-in protection are up to date and switched on
  • Show you what to watch for next time

What it costs

Diagnosis is free and most jobs fall between $30 and $80 for the work. Parts, if you need any, are quoted separately. See the full price list. You always get a clear quote before anything starts, and nothing happens until you approve it.

A warning about scam pop-ups

If a pop-up or a caller says your computer is infected and asks for remote access or payment, don't call the number and don't let them in. Microsoft doesn't contact people that way. Close the browser, and if someone already got access, turn the computer off and get in touch. It's also worth calling your bank.

How long it takes

Most virus removals are done within 1 to 3 days, depending on how deep the infection goes. If you need the computer back sooner, say so when you book and I'll tell you honestly whether that's doable.

Questions people ask

Do I need to buy antivirus software?

For most people at home, no. Windows Security, which comes built into Windows 10 and 11, does a solid job when it's switched on and kept up to date. I'll make sure yours is, so you're not paying for a subscription you don't need.

What if the infection is really bad?

Sometimes the cleanest fix is to back up your files and reinstall Windows from scratch. If that's the case, I'll explain why and quote it before doing anything.

Should I change my passwords?

If you typed passwords on the infected computer, yes. Once it's clean, change the important ones first, starting with your email and online banking, ideally from a different device.
